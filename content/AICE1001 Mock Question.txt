Evelyn and Leo, the founders of NextGen Robotics, have just completed their latest project: MechaMates, an advanced robot companion that can assist with household tasks, answer questions about various topics, and even play interactive games. They're thrilled to launch MechaMates to consumers worldwide, but some unexpected issues have arisen.
First, a week after launching, Evelyn and Leo realised they overlooked the terms in a contract they signed with a supplier who provided some of the robotic hardware. Now they're concerned they might have breached the contract terms without realising it.
Additionally, one of their early customers, Alex Tran, discovered that MechaMates can be manipulated into performing unauthorised tasks if prompted in certain ways. MechaMates doesn't fully recognise trick questions and will sometimes execute commands it shouldn't exposing a potential security risk.
To complicate matters, NextGen Robotics has received a cease-and-desist letter from an inventor claiming that MechaMates infringes on their patent for a game-playing robot. Evelyn and Leo aren't sure if the claim is legitimate, but they're anxious to avoid a legal dispute. 
Lastly, Evelyn has been telling Leo that they need better cybersecurity in their office, especially after hearing about recent data breaches at other tech companies. However, Leo believes that because they store little sensitive data, cybersecurity isn't a major concern.
